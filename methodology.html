1. The architectural choice

Start with a strong, fixed composition. Preserve its photographic detail, then spend rendering work on water, changing light, leaves, mist and occasional animals. This is a 2.5D scene, not a reconstructed 3D forest. Its composited “stickers” have names, anchors, scale, depth and actions; a sticker need not look like paper or have an illustrated outline.

The architectural change is separating what exists, where it belongs, what it is doing, who chooses its next action, and how it is drawn. The forest is the first test case, not the limit. A future city could reuse the control/lifecycle pattern while supplying new assets, paths, actions and rendering adapters.

GitHub Pages serves ordinary static files; the visitor's browser does the work. No backend, streaming video, remote inference, framework, dependencies or build step is required. The implementation now lives at the MAP root. The former forest demo route is removed, not redirected. Relative asset/import paths support both local / and deployed /MAP/.

Implemented: an in-page control boundary and a seeded local ambient director. Not implemented: an AI agent, authenticated agent service, multiplayer, persistence, general world editor or arbitrary-script loader. Those future systems can use this boundary without owning the rendering loop.

3. A small, inspectable direction protocol

The page exposes window.stickerWorld. Integrations should observe → choose → dispatch → inspect receipt → observe again. The optional Direct scene panel uses the same route for people. The runtime contract includes exact exports, object IDs, argument bounds and copyable examples.

A command names an id, object, action, optional bounded args and optional expectedRevision. Types, objects, actions, paths, numeric bounds and state constraints are checked before mutation. Unknown paths do not become arbitrary routes. Command data cannot supply code, asset URLs, network targets or a renderer.

observe() returns detached state. Receipts report success/failure, a rejection reason where relevant and revision. Successful IDs are cached in a 128-entry window: an identical retry returns its original receipt; a changed payload with the same ID fails with id-conflict. Rejections do not mutate state. This is bounded retry protection, not durable exactly-once delivery; an evicted ID may be accepted again.

expectedRevision checks accepted-command and lifecycle changes. On a stale revision, observe and reconsider instead of blindly retrying. History is capped at 64 entries, not a persistent event store. Reloading creates a new local world.

The seeded ambient director also sends validated commands. Repeatable local scheduling is useful for tests but is not AI. A future remote adapter needs authority, rate limits and arbitration before several people or services can share the scene safely.

An accepted manual/external command switches off new ambient choices; active actions finish. The visitor can resume the director explicitly. New commands are rejected while paused; the page adapter also rejects them while hidden or without animated graphics. This in-page API is an integration contract, not a security boundary against other JavaScript already on the page.

4. One clock, many kinds of movement

scene.js owns the presentation loop and advances scene time only while motion is enabled. The world, policy and renderers share it. Stickers do not create independent loops. Animal events carry stable IDs; audio deduplicates them.

Daylight is a separate value: the 06:00–19:30 slider changes light without speeding wingbeats. Redundant preset buttons are removed. “Let the day pass” advances the daylight span over 180 seconds, continues from the selected hour and stops at dusk. Rain is independent.

These are distinct, addressable behaviors—not physical simulation, animal intelligence or a general collision system. Pause holds the shared clock; reduced motion begins paused. Hidden pages suspend animation/audio and reset their wall-time baseline on return. Paused or hidden intervals must not accumulate missed calls. Sound needs a gesture. Without JavaScript or WebGL, retain a static image.

5. Author in image coordinates

Use normalized source-image positions: (0, 0) is top-left, (1, 1) is bottom-right. Put paths, anchors, water boundaries and light openings there. Every layer shares cover cropping, 2.5% overscan and pointer offset; viewport-percentage masks would separate from the image when cropped.

cover = [min(1, viewportAspect / imageAspect),
         min(1, imageAspect / viewportAspect)]
sourcePoint = (viewportPoint - 0.5) * cover / overscan
              + 0.5 + pointerOffset

Keep the WebGL texture-orientation conversion at the sampling boundary. Use the inverse mapping for the sticker canvas. Check a known anchor in landscape and portrait before tuning motion. scene-config.js supplies the common 1672/941 aspect, 1.025 overscan and material settings; the manifest supplies instances and paths.

The 2 × 2 parrot atlas has three flight poses and a perched pose. Measure alpha bounds on load, share flight crops and register each torso so the body does not jump with its wings. Anchor a perched bird at its feet. Match focus, cutout fringes, scale, contrast and lighting to the scene; a reusable sticker still needs art direction.

The diffuse neutral forest plate has no visible sun or baked directional beams. The browser supplies changing rays, dapple, warmth, exposure and water highlights. The old sunlit plate is provenance, not a runtime request. Neutral is not unlit: diffuse shading and reflections remain. Without geometry or surface normals, this is not ray tracing, physical relighting or an astronomical model.

6. Sound belongs to events and distance

Filtered noise supplies a low-level stream, leaf and rain bed. Synthesized animal cues have stable IDs, stereo position, distance attenuation and low-pass filtering. At most three transient voices may sound, with a minimum 0.8-second global cue gap and three seconds between parrot cues. Deduplicate events and release each voice when it ends.

Start quietly, leave headroom and fade transitions. Repeated Sound toggles must not create overlapping contexts or calls. Browser gains are relative, not calibrated sound-pressure levels; the listener controls actual volume.

This is procedural synthesis, not field recording or scientifically accurate species calls. It is not documentary evidence. Agent direction must not bypass sound opt-in, volume, pause or reduced-motion preferences.

7. Spend explicit budgets

BudgetPolicy—not a guarantee
PresentationAt most 30 rendered frames per second, about 33.3 ms apart.
WebGL surfaceInitially 1.2 million pixels, or 0.65 million on narrow/coarse-pointer devices; floor 0.36 million.
DrawingOne WebGL scene pass and a small transparent sticker canvas; reuse textures and decoded atlases.
Idle/audioNo settled paused/hidden render loop; sound opt-in, bounded voices and hidden-page suspension.
CommandsAllowlisted actions, bounded receipts/history, no arbitrary code or per-frame network calls.

The quality controller checks 90 positive frame intervals. If over 22% exceed 48 ms, it multiplies the cap by 0.76, with a 360,000-pixel floor and at least six scene seconds between reductions. Quality stays reduced until reload. Reset the hidden/resume baseline; count very slow foreground frames so overloaded devices can reduce resolution.

About → Rendering details reports observed cadence, internal dimensions, cap and average CPU submission. One-off manual draws do not inflate the counter. CPU submission is not GPU completion time. Keep blocking graphics queries/readbacks out of production frames. Test physical target devices before claiming mobile frame rate or battery efficiency.

8. Replicate and extend

  1. Choose a fixed view and readable action space. A strong plate needs depth and room for motion. A busy forest may hide useful behavior; a street, room or plaza could be a clearer interaction test.
  2. Prepare neutral art and a small asset set. Record provenance and full prompts. Avoid baked beams for relighting. Export compressed alternatives and inspect alpha edges.
  3. Separate types from instances. Put reusable action metadata in the catalog, named placements/paths in a manifest. A parrot type must not contain an entire forest route.
  4. Author registration and constraints. Set anchors, depth, paths and contact points. Reauthor masks for each plate. Check portrait and landscape.
  5. Implement one behavior at a time. Distinguish pose changes, travel, deformation, contact, flow and event audio. More amplitude is not a new kind of movement.
  6. Keep policy outside rendering. Direct through bounded commands. Observe first, use unique IDs, inspect receipts and handle stale revisions. Never inject executable callbacks from command data.
  7. Honor visitor controls. Preserve pause, reduced motion, audio consent, visibility and static fallback. Future agents do not implicitly outrank these preferences.
  8. Test, measure and deploy. Run deterministic checks, browser review and real-device measurements. Verify relative URLs at the Pages project prefix.

Next steps—not implemented here

  • Versioned asset/library packaging, provenance checks and manifest migration.
  • Generalized occlusion, contact surfaces, path affordances and action composition beyond the forest adapter.
  • An agent transport adapter with explicit authority, rate limits and approval policy.
  • Shared sessions, permissions, arbitration, persistence and durable replay. GitHub Pages alone provides none of these services.
  • A second scene that tests reuse with new actions, rather than only a new background.

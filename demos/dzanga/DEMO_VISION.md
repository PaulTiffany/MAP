# Demo vision — a living rainforest surface

## Thesis

The demo should feel less like a website than entering an illustrated rainforest that can explain itself.

The user begins above an apparently continuous green world. Panning reveals geography. Zooming changes what kind of thing the world is: canopy becomes routes and clearings; routes become traces, roots, water, and animal movement; close inspection reveals small interactive systems. Entering a guided trail turns ordinary scroll into an authored camera-and-graphics performance without leaving the same world.

## Why Dzanga-Sangha

The Sangha Trinational landscape is a connected mosaic of humid tropical forest, swamp forest, streams, rivers, and natural forest clearings. Dzanga-Sangha is especially useful as a visual reference because the dense closed canopy is punctuated by bais — mineral-rich openings that gather animals and make otherwise hidden relations visible.

That geography maps naturally onto the platform:

- **canopy** → macro semantic layer;
- **paths / river / bais** → structural layer;
- **tracks / roots / seed movement / water** → mechanism layer;
- **tiny organisms / annotations / controls** → deep semantic layer.

## Experience target

A visitor should be able to do two things without changing applications:

1. **wander** — pan and zoom freely through a coherent fantastical rainforest;
2. **follow** — enter a trail where scrolling scrubs an authored sequence through that same geography.

The guided sequence must never feel like a stack of panels hidden inside a map. The world itself changes.

## Signature moments

1. **Canopy breath** — fast scroll produces a short-lived wave through nearby canopy masses; stopping lets the forest settle.
2. **River reveal** — a broad river seen from far away resolves into braided channels and small ripples as scale increases.
3. **Bai opening** — fog and canopy visually part while the camera approaches a clearing; the clearing brightens instead of hard-cutting into a new scene.
4. **Path memory** — a moving marker leaves a transient trace along an animal corridor as progress advances.
5. **Understory inversion** — at deep zoom the dominant canopy becomes translucent and a root/fungal/water network becomes the primary image.
6. **Semantic return** — zooming back out recomposes the detailed systems into geography without a reset.

## Success criteria

The slice succeeds when:

- semantic zoom is perceptibly more than magnification;
- scroll position, zoom depth, and scroll velocity visibly affect the same world in different ways;
- free roam and guided choreography can be entered/exited without discontinuity;
- the first useful frame appears quickly on GitHub Pages;
- the SVG remains inspectable enough to author with semantic IDs;
- reducing motion preserves meaning rather than merely disabling the experience;
- the reference to Central African rainforest ecology is recognizable without turning BaAka people into scenery.

## Future benchmark

After the rainforest demo is convincing, build a rigid future-city demo against the same engine. Organic terrain hides different classes of error than architecture; both are needed to prove generality.

# Forest style

## Overall character

**Illustrated ecological atlas + humid dream + precise instrument.**

The world should feel hand-drawn at first glance and computational under inspection. Avoid game HUD chrome, neon cyberpunk, generic fantasy runes, or photorealistic jungle wallpaper.

## Palette

Base families rather than fixed colors:

- deep canopy: near-black green;
- wet leaf: blue-green;
- young growth: yellow-green;
- river: muted blue / slate;
- bai: warm clay, pale mineral water, soft grasses;
- mist: cool gray-green;
- signal / active progress: restrained amber;
- night-detail / hidden network: dim cyan-green, never neon.

The palette should remain readable in grayscale through value contrast.

## Geometry

### Macro

- broad interlocking canopy masses;
- meandering Sangha-inspired river geometry;
- irregular clearings rather than circles;
- subtle topographic / hydrographic contour marks.

### Mid

- buttress-root silhouettes;
- elephant-width paths that curve and fork;
- swamp pools and braided streams;
- vine arcs and canopy gaps;
- clustered tree crowns with asymmetric spacing.

### Close

- roots, fungi, insects, water rings, leaf veins;
- track marks and seed traces;
- tiny observation marks / ecological annotations;
- no decorative pseudo-writing.

## Semantic zoom language

### 0.25–0.65× — canopy / geography

See mass, water, clearings, and major corridors. Individual trees are texture.

### 0.55–1.25× — relations

Canopy clusters separate. Paths, bais, river branches, animal routes, and wind fields appear.

### 1.0–2.4× — mechanisms

Root zones, seed corridors, water flow, footprints, and local ecological interactions become primary.

### 2.0×+ — micro interface

Fine tracery, small labels, inspectable nodes, and embedded controls appear. Detail should replace visual density rather than simply adding more ink.

## Motion language

Motion belongs to one of four families:

1. **Breath** — slow deformation / opacity drift linked to ambient time or user activity.
2. **Flow** — river, fog, seeds, animals, particles following paths.
3. **Reveal** — path drawing, canopy parting, root emergence, semantic cross-fades.
4. **Response** — short impulses driven by scroll speed/direction, then decay.

Never run everything at once. At most one motion family should dominate a region at a time.

## Scroll response

Scroll should feel like force applied to an ecosystem, not like a video scrub bar.

- low velocity: precise reveals and subtle path motion;
- medium velocity: canopy sway and fog displacement become perceptible;
- high velocity: brief larger response, but never a particle explosion;
- stop: all velocity-coupled effects decay smoothly back to rest.

## Audio

No audio in v1. Silence makes the visual engine easier to evaluate and avoids casual appropriation of BaAka musical material. If sound is later added, begin with original environmental synthesis, not cultural recordings.
